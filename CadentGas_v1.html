01. UX Research & Immersion

Understanding the Physiology of Crisis

Before touching wireframes, we shadowed agents during peak hours (6:00 AM – 10:00 AM, the "gas-ignition window"). Under extreme stress, working memory shrinks and visual attention narrows.

The Hover Phenomenon

Agents hovered over fields for an average of 2.3 seconds before typing—reading micro-text (10-11px) labels repeatedly while trying to listen to distressed callers.

The Repetition Spiral

Navigating away to verify postcodes caused 68% of callers to restart their issue description from the beginning, adding 90–120 seconds to call duration.

The Critical Pivot Point

In 73% of calls, caller distress peaked within the first 90 seconds while confirming postcodes. Lack of address fallback caused 8–14 seconds of dead silence.

02. Information Architecture

Restructuring Mental Models (17 Emergency Types)

Through closed card sorting with 8 agents, we reorganized the flat, alphabetical 17-type dropdown into 4 life-critical priority clusters that match natural conversational triage.

Priority 1

Life-Threat Emergency

Immediate 999 co-dispatch. Mandatory evacuation scripts.

  • Fire / Explosion
  • Internal Gas Escape
  • External Gas Escape
  • Carbon Monoxide
Priority 2

Supply & Pressure

Urgent response (2-hour ETA). Restores supply grid.

  • No Gas / Low Pressure
  • ECV Valve Problem
  • Purge & Relight
  • Concern for Safety
Priority 3

Appliance & Network

Scheduled engineer visit for specific appliance issues.

  • Appliance Stuck On
  • Animal / Object Behind
  • Pipework Survey
  • Site Works Request
Priority 4

Administrative

Back-office routing with zero field dispatch needed.

  • Meter Enquiries
  • Reportable Issues
  • Theft of Gas
